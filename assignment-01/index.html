My Top 5 Favorite Video Games

These five games stand out to me because they offer different ways to explore, solve problems, and enjoy a challenge.

  1. Minecraft

    Minecraft takes first place because it lets me decide what a successful play session looks like. I can gather materials and build a shelter in Survival mode, explore underground caves, or work on a large building in Creative mode. Its block-based world makes simple ideas easy to start, but projects can become surprisingly complex as I learn more. I enjoy having the freedom to set my own goals and turn an empty area into something that feels personal.

  2. Grand Theft Auto V

    Grand Theft Auto V combines driving, action missions, and exploration across Los Santos and the surrounding countryside. Switching between its three main characters gives the story different perspectives and keeps the missions varied. It earns second place because I enjoy the contrast between carefully planned heists and the unpredictable moments that happen while traveling around the map. Even taking a break from the story can lead to an entertaining drive or a new place to explore.

  3. Red Dead Redemption 2

    Red Dead Redemption 2 follows Arthur Morgan as he struggles with loyalty and survival in a changing American West. The gameplay mixes horseback travel, gunfights, hunting, and conversations with people throughout the world. I rank it third because its slower pace gives the characters and setting room to matter. Riding back to camp after a mission or stopping to investigate a stranger can be as memorable as a major story event, which makes the journey feel worth paying attention to.

  4. Fortnite

    Fortnite turns a simple goal—being the last player or team standing—into matches that demand quick decisions. Finding equipment, choosing when to fight, and moving safely as the playable area shrinks all affect the outcome. I place it fourth because I like the teamwork involved in sharing supplies and coordinating with friends. Its building mechanics add another skill to practice, while Zero Build offers a different challenge focused on movement, cover, and positioning.

  5. The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild

    The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild gives Link a large version of Hyrule to explore through climbing, gliding, combat, and puzzle solving. Many obstacles allow more than one solution, so experimenting with tools and the environment often pays off. It belongs in my top five because I enjoy spotting an interesting landmark and figuring out how to reach it without following a fixed route. The shrines provide focused challenges along the way, making exploration feel both open-ended and rewarding.
